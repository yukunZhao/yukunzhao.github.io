## **TEACHING STATEMENT** 

Yukun Zhao (yukunzhao.sdu@gmail.com) 

After nearly five years working at Baidu and several years of academic training, I have come to deeply appreciate the dual impact that comes from bridging academia and industry. As an engineer and research leader in industry, my work has reached billions of users through real-world applications. But as a mentor and educator, I have found equal fulfillment in shaping the growth of individuals, watching students and junior engineers mature into independent researchers and skilled engineers. The passion for mentorship and education drives me to pursue a faculty position at Shandong University. 

**Mentoring Experience.** My mentorship experience spans both academic and industrial settings. While working at Baidu and during my doctoral training at Shandong University, I had the opportunity to mentor three Ph.D. students and one undergraduate student on research projects in natural language processing and information retrieval. These collaborations led to multiple publications at top-tier conferences, including NAACL and AAAI. In addition to technical guidance, I coached students on research problems, methodology, critical thinking, and scientific writing, helping them gain the confidence and independence needed for academic success. At Baidu, where I have worked as a staff engineer and team leader in the Search Algorithm Department since 2020, I have mentored over a dozen junior algorithm engineers. Many of them have successfully advanced to senior roles under my guidance. My mentorship style focuses not only on technical growth, such as model optimization, system deployment, but also on broader career development, including presentation skills, cross-team communication, and leadership training. These experiences have reinforced my commitment to long-term, holistic mentorship and to cultivating an environment where mentees can thrive. 

These dual experiences have equipped me with strong leadership and communication skills, as well as a deep appreciation for the power of education. I firmly believe that effective teaching goes beyond delivering content—it involves inspiring students to take ownership of their learning and equipping them with the skills to make meaningful contributions in both academia and industry. 

**Teaching Philosophy.** My teaching philosophy is shaped by my journey as a student, researcher, mentor, and engineer. It is grounded in three core principles: (1) Motivation: Students learn best when they understand what they are studying. Drawing on my experience at Baidu, JD.com, and Alibaba, I strive to connect theoretical concepts with real-world applications—whether in search or recommendation. I design assignments and projects that reflect practical challenges to make learning engaging and impactful; (2) Mentorship and Individual Growth: I view teaching as a deeply personal process. Every student has unique strengths and goals, and I aim to provide individualized mentorship that fosters self-confidence, critical thinking, and problem-solving skills. I encourage independent exploration while offering structured guidance and constructive feedback. 

**Teaching Interests.** With my academic and industrial background in natural language processing, machine learning, and information retrieval, I am prepared to teach a wide range of undergraduate and graduate-level courses. Specifically, I am well-suited to offer (1) introductory and advanced courses in natural language processing, large language models, and information retrieval. (2) Application-focused courses such as Multimodal Search Systems, question answering systems, and Applied AI in Industry. 

I am also eager to participate in student mentorship programs, undergraduate research initiatives, and curriculum development efforts. 

In summary, my experiences in both academia and industry have shaped me into a passionate and thoughtful educator. I am excited about the opportunity to contribute to Shandong University’s academic mission by teaching, mentoring, and building strong bridges between theory and practice.
